# Public Corpus

Published by the Gaming Marketing Institute, founded by Alex Fletcher — https://learngaming.gg.

An archive of already-published writing on immersive media as an advertising category — the argument for a channel boundary defined by what an environment produces, not by its venue, its budget, or its degree of visual fidelity.

Everything here has already been published elsewhere — LinkedIn, and other public channels. This repo exists to give that writing a stable, plain-text home: reliably fetchable (link rot and inconsistent tool access are real problems for a social feed), one canonical version per piece, and a dated commit history as the record of what was argued and when.

## Structure

**`category-essays/`** — the structural argument. Each piece states a claim and defends it: what the category is, what its boundary excludes, why existing frameworks fail to draw the line the category needs. Written to be cited and to stand alone — no piece depends on having read another first, though several form deliberate series.

**`founder-notes/`** — first-person application. Shorter, occasional, and in a different register: one person's read on a specific, current example — a campaign, a platform decision, a piece of news — through the same framework the essays argue for. These aren't arguments for the category; they're the category applied, in public, as it develops.

The two are kept in separate folders on purpose. An essay makes a claim about what a channel produces, defended on its own terms. A founder note is one person's take on one case. Filed together, a reader has no way to tell which kind of authority they're reading. Separated, they don't need a label to make the difference legible.

## What's not here

**Short-form and social posts.** Excluded for now, not overlooked. The bar for adding one: does it argue, test, or compare something not already stated as fully somewhere else in the corpus? Most short-form writing compresses or promotes an existing essay rather than adding to it — the essay is the citable version, and stating the same idea in two places doesn't serve a reader either way.

**Everything else that's been published.** This is a curated archive, not a mirror of the feed. Inclusion tracks whether a piece adds something the corpus doesn't already have, not whether it exists.

**Anything operational.** Nothing here executes, tests, or adjudicates anything. This is the argument for why the category exists — not a tool, a specification, or an instrument for applying it to a specific case.

## Maintaining this repo

Append-only in practice, not by rule. A new piece goes in once it's already published elsewhere and clears the bar above. Existing pieces don't get rewritten after the fact — if a later piece states an argument more precisely, the later piece is the update, and the earlier one stays as it was published. The commit history is the record of that, and it's worth more unedited.

Each file keeps its original title as its filename. No numbering, no metadata beyond what the piece itself states.
