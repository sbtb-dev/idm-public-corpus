# The Funnel A Brand Has Nowhere Else

A non-endemic (non-gaming) brand carries no behavioral record of pre-conversion response anywhere outside immersive media's boundary. Inside a digital game, the native numbers look like the record it was missing. They belong to the game itself, and the boundary that governs the immersive media channel refuses to let the brand count them as its own. That refusal is not a limitation of the channel. It is the reason the channel produces actionable evidence.

## The gap is real, and it is not an audience gap
A non-endemic brand can reach gaming audiences everywhere. "Gamers" exist on every single platform, in every channel. Native interest targeting is sold at scale; the population is trivially reachable in a feed for pennies. Reach was never the missing thing.

What is missing is behavioral evidence of response before the transaction. Between exposure and purchase — encounter, engagement, preference, intent — every conventional channel a brand runs falls back on proxies. Reach, frequency, modeled attention, and stated recall are stand-ins for consumer response, not consumer response. Display observes exposure and infers the rest. Search observes intent already formed. Social observes engagement with content about the brand. None of them observes the brand-directed behavior itself, because none of them is built where it could.

That's the gap. It is a gap in the response evidence, not in the audience, and it exists on every channel a non-endemic brand has ever bought.

## Inside the game, the numbers look like the fix
An immersive environment is dense with behavioral data. Playtime, session duration, engagement rate, retention, item interaction — the numbers are rich, they are behavioral, and they are right there, produced continuously by the surface the brand activated on.

To a brand that has only ever had proxies at these stages, those numbers read as the record it was always missing. Here at last is behavior, captured in the moment, at the funnel stages every other channel covers with inference. The temptation is to treat the environment's native numbers as the brand's pre-conversion evidence. That is the error the boundary exists to catch.

## Native numbers are the game's funnel, not the brand's
Native numbers measure the game's own surface, and they are attributed to the party that runs it. That party is the operator — the studio, the publisher, the creator behind the game. A free-to-play live-service operator observes player behavior at activation, engagement, retention, and monetization because the commercial model has no revenue outside the funnel it observes. That instrumentation is load-bearing for the operator's own business, and it records the surface's pull on the player. This is the game's own funnel, and every number in it belongs to the operator.

Playtime is the operator's number. It measures how well the environment holds the player, which is the operator's question, answered by native instrumentation. A brand, inserted into an immersive environment, that reads playtime as its own evidence has read the operator's funnel and filed it incorrectly. The behavior is real; the attribution is wrong. A non-branded equivalent in the same role could have drawn the same time-in-environment, so the number says nothing about the brand's presence specifically.

The point is structural. A surface that captures rich, funnel-tight native behavior does not qualify a brand's demand by that richness. The instrumentation observes the surface's own pull and attributes the response to the operator that organizes it. Volume of captured behavior, proximity to purchase, and native-funnel tightness are not the gate.

## The operator's funnel converts, and every dollar of it is the operator's
The operator funnel does not end at a metric. It ends at a transaction, and the transactions are enormous. Consumer spending on games is approaching $200 billion a year — one of the largest consumer-spend totals in media, and nearly all of it is money players spend on games and inside them, not advertising placed against them.

That total is the operator funnel's output. It is player behavior converted into player spend. The number proves the funnel works, and it proves the funnel is the operator's. Every dollar of it is demand for the game, captured by the operator that runs it.

Read from a non-endemic brand's seat, that is the whole argument stated at its largest. The richest, most converting funnel in media produces a figure the brand has no claim on, because the funnel observes the player's relationship to the game, and the brand is not the game. The biggest number in the room is brand-unattributed by construction.

So the scale of the operator funnel is not an invitation to share it. It is the reason a brand needs its own. What a non-endemic brand wants is not a slice of spend on games — it is demand for its own products, and evidence of that demand it can act on. That demand runs through a different funnel, and the operator's converting billions do nothing to produce it.

## The brand-presence funnel is a different funnel
The funnel a brand needs is not the operator's funnel observed harder. It is a different funnel, observed at the same grade the operator's instrumentation already proves possible.

The brand-presence funnel is the consumer's response to the brand specifically: clicking the brand object by choice, returning to it repeatedly, choosing it over a generic alternative, queuing for the branded drop. Response to the brand's presence is the funnel; response to the environment is not. The two run on the same surface and are not the same record.

The line between them is the control. A response occurring on a branded surface is not yet a response to the brand — the object of the behavior has to be established, by isolating the brand-attributed response against a non-branded equivalent in the same role. Isolation is the gate, not richness. A guest brand activated inside a rich environment produces evidence only once its response is separated from the surface's own pull.

## The boundary does not force a conversion 
Here is the correction that matters most, because it is where the intuitive version of this argument goes wrong.

Immersive media's boundary does not turn player behavior into brand-attributed behavior. It refuses that conversion. It disqualifies the inherited player number and requires the brand's own record in its place. Player behavior stays player behavior. The brand does not receive the operator's funnel relabeled; it receives a separate funnel it had to produce.

nheritance is the exact error the immersive media standard prevents. A branded game built on a hosting platform (like Roblox) inherits the platform's general capacity to capture player behavior — and that is not brand-attributed behavioral response by default. The activation surface either produces the response evidence or it does not. The environment's capacity never hands the activation its qualification.

So the boundary is not a bridge between the operator's funnel and the brand's. Nothing carries across from one funnel to the other. The brand's funnel is not assembled from the operator's; it is produced on its own or it is absent.

## Refusal is the value
The refusal is what makes the resulting evidence worth having. If the operator's numbers counted as the brand's evidence, the brand would hold a record it cannot act on, because it could not separate the environment's pull from the effect of its own presence. A number that conflates the two is not a weaker version of brand-attributed evidence. It is not a suitable class of evidence at all.

By refusing the conversion, the standard forces the activation to produce a record whose object is unambiguous: the brand's presence, and the response to it, isolated from the surface that hosts it. A record a brand can act on is a record the operator's funnel could never have supplied. The standard's severity is the source of the evidence's value, not a tax on it.

## The case, in support
Structurally, the failure looks like counting the wrong funnel. Renault Group placed the Renault 5 E-Tech across a range of game worlds — Fortnite, Minecraft, Roblox, and others — through community modders, who built the car into each title in that title's own idiom, reaching players inside spaces where they already were.

Several of those worlds run on live-service infrastructure that observes player behavior as a condition of operating. The capacity to produce brand-attributed response evidence was present in the venue. The activation reported none of it, and recorded its success as conversation — coverage in gaming press, spread through streamer play-throughs, volume of discussion generated outside the games. 

The activation ran inside environments capable of observing consumer response to the brand directly and was measured entirely by response that happened elsewhere. The funnel was available; Renault counted a different one.

## What remains
Everywhere a non-endemic brand operates, the pre-conversion stages are covered by proxies, because the behavioral funnel was never observable there. Inside a qualifying immersive environment, it is — not as the operator's numbers borrowed and relabeled, but as the brand's own response funnel, isolated and observed at operator grade.

That is the funnel a brand has nowhere else. The channel does not bridge the gap; it produces the record the gap was made of. The native metrics are the surface's efficiency, attributed to the operator. The brand's evidence is a separate thing the boundary demands the activation produce, and the demand is the reason the evidence can be trusted.
