# The Gaming Line Is a Blank Check, Part 3: The One Line That Is Not Blank

*One part of gaming supply earns a line of its own. It earns that line by what the buy returns, not by where it runs, and it is the only line from the bundle with a price check built in.*

---

## Where the series stands

The series' first part argued that a gaming line is a blank check. Anything with the gaming label can enter it. Its checks ask whether gaming worked, against gaming's own bar, and nothing compares the price with the same unit elsewhere. The second part set out the rule that replaces it:

> Each format goes in the line where the brand buys the same unit from every other seller. A new line earns its place only when being in it predicts the unit, the terms, and the return.

CTV passes the second part. A gaming line fails it at any width. Part two left one item open: a brand's role inside play. This part shows why that item passes the second part, why the line it earns is not a gaming line, and why it is the one item from the bundle that is not a blank check.

## The item with nowhere to go

The first part of the rule sorts almost everything. Rewarded video goes to the video line. A placement in a retailer's gaming hub goes to retail media. A creator buy goes to creator and social. Each of these units is also sold by sellers outside gaming, so each has a native line to return to.

One item does not: the brand as part of the world. Examples are an item a player equips, a zone a player enters, or a mechanic a player uses. Platforms and endemic vendors sell this unit today. But the same unit cannot be sold outside an environment that people inhabit. So no line in the plan recognizes it, and it is sold on partnership terms. The first part of the rule cannot place it.

The IAB's own format table reaches the same remainder. It gives each gaming ad format at least one standard media type, such as display, video, or audio. The one exception is custom integrations. Their media type is listed only as "Custom," and their measures start with "Custom KPIs" ([IAB](https://www.iab.com/guidelines/the-business-case-for-gaming/), Appendix D).

That leaves two places for it. It can stay on the partnership line, where most of these builds sit today: bought on association, and reported with whatever the venue counted after the run. Or it can earn a new line. A new line must pass the second part of the rule.

## How it passes

A line defined by where an item runs fails the second part. Being in a game predicts nothing about the unit, the terms, or the return. A line defined by what the buy returns can pass, if the order names these terms before the build:

- **The stages:** the four pre-conversion stages, encounter response, engagement, preference, and intent, each written as a behavior on the brand's surface in the environment's own grammar. A zone entered can be an encounter response. An item equipped is intent. The order does not select a stage. The buy must be able to produce all four, and it must report all four.
- **The exposed population and the alternative:** everyone the buy reached, including the people who did not engage, and an alternative on the surface that people can choose instead of the brand. A reading of participants alone would credit the buy with the preference that made people take part.
- **An unbranded twin:** the same surface and mechanic, in the same role, with matched traffic, in the same environment, with no brand. Neither arm carries a reward. A reward attached to the presence measures the reward, not the brand.
- **The window:** the period over which the record is read.
- **The verifier:** the party that can check the record, and the form in which the record is delivered.

The record then gives two readings, and they are not one effect. First-encounter response against the twin shows what the brand carried in. Movement after first encounter, beyond the twin's trajectory, shows what this buy did. Only the second reading is credited to the buy. If the two were merged, a strong brand would make every buy look good, and a buy could never get a "no."

Working media on this line buys what it buys on any other line: the brand's presence, placed in front of people. The build that makes the presence possible is production, as it is for a TV spot. What is different is how the spend earns its place in the plan. Most lines earn working-media spend through a currency, a count that the market accepts as proof that the buy ran.

A brand's role inside play has no currency, and it cannot get one, because no two worlds sell the same unit. That is why it has stayed on partnership terms. The record gives it the other route into the plan. Each buy shows, in what people did, whether the presence moved them toward the brand. Spend that shows that can be judged, and spend that can be judged can sit in the plan as working media.

With those terms in the order, being in the line predicts all three things the rule asks for. The unit is the brand's presence, placed in front of a named population and read against an unbranded twin, at the four stages, over a named window. The terms are written before the run, and any seller can be asked to meet them. The return is a record, checked by a named party, of how the people the buy reached responded to the brand before the sale. Among the lines brand spend can buy, no other line returns that record as part of the buy.

The way it passes is different from CTV. CTV passed because the environment changed the terms. This line passes because the order does. The environment is necessary, because only a world that records behavior can produce the record. But the environment is not sufficient. A build with no twin in the order stays on the partnership line, however good its numbers are. The full case is in "The Immersive Line Is Written at the Order."

## It is not a gaming line

The strongest objection to this part is that the rule has let the gaming line back in through a side door. It has not, for three reasons.

**Membership is per surface, not per venue.** Take one Roblox world that a host owns. A branded item equipped, read against an unbranded twin, goes on this line. Other branded surfaces in the same world, with no twin, go on the partnership line. A rewarded-video slot the platform sells in the same world goes on the video line. One venue holds three lines, and nothing passes between them. If the brand builds the world itself, the build is a production cost. It is not media on any of these lines.

**Most gaming supply never enters.** Rewarded video, in-game display, playables, esports sponsorship, creator buys, and retailer hubs carry no twin in the order. Each one stays in its native line. Being in a game does not qualify an item. The order does.

**The label brings nothing in.** Part 2 showed that the gaming label carries a sales case: games are high-attention environments. That case does not qualify anything for this line, because attention is not the unit. If the line were defined by gaming, the attention case would qualify an item. Because it is defined by what the buy returns, the attention case qualifies nothing.

For these reasons the line needs its own name. The channel is immersive media, and its place in the plan is the immersive line. Gaming supplies most of the worlds that can produce its record today, but the line belongs to the record, not to gaming.

## The one line with a price check

Part 1 defined the blank check exactly. The gaming line checks outcomes, but it has no price check. Cost discipline compares each unit with the same unit elsewhere, and inside a gaming line no unit has a peer.

The immersive line has a price check. Its unit is written in the order, so any seller can be asked to bid on the same specification: the same four stages, the same twin, the same population, and the same window. Within one environment, two bids on that specification can be compared directly. That is the comparison the gaming line could never make.

Across environments, the specification stays the same, but the result does not. The twin cancels each world's draw. It does not remove the fit between the brand and that world. So a second world is a second test, not a second quote for the same result. What travels between worlds is the specification and the track record, not the finding.

The twin also links Part 3 to Part 2's tests. In Part 2, a brand tests each native line against a holdout group that does not see the ads, which is the method the IAB's own expert recommended. The twin does the job of a holdout in a different way. A holdout removes the exposure. The twin keeps the exposure, and holds the surface, the mechanic, the role, and the traffic constant, and removes only the brand. Holdout tests on other lines read behavior at conversion. The twin reads it before the sale. Other lines buy media and then test it. This line puts the test in the order.

## What changes in the plan

The series ends in three decisions:

1. **Spend on gaming wherever a format earns its place.** The industry is right about that.
2. **Do not open a gaming line.** Sort every item sold as gaming by its unit, into the line where the brand buys that unit from every other seller, and compare it there.
3. **For a brand's role inside play, ask two questions before the build:** what is the unit, and what is the twin? With no unit in the order, the build goes on the partnership line. With a unit but no twin, it goes in the native line for that record, as rich media or, with a closed outcome, as performance. With a unit and an unbranded twin, it goes on the immersive line, and the record judges it.

The gaming line asked brands to fund a label and to trust the price. The immersive line asks them to fund the brand's presence and to judge it by its record. Among the lines brand spend can buy, no other line returns that record as part of the buy.
