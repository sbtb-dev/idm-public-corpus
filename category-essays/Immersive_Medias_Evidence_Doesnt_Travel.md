# Immersive Media's Evidence Doesn't Travel

Immersive media is defined by the evidence it produces inside its own environment. The qualifying evidence is direct behavioral response produced on the surface the brand's presence organizes, captured by the environment's native instrumentation, recorded as one unified account at pre-conversion funnel stages.

That evidence is native to the environment. It is produced in place or not at all. Native evidence does not travel — it cannot be produced in another context and carried in to stand for response the brand's presence never organized. Other channels — social, display, video — reconstruct response from signals captured elsewhere because they cannot produce the same native evidence. Immersive has it. Importing a figure from another context to stand in its place forfeits the one thing the channel exists to provide.

That evidence has a position it is meant to fill. The response slot is the place in an activation's case where its response evidence must sit. Every immersive activation makes one claim — that the brand's presence produced consumer response — and the slot is where the figure backing that claim goes. A figure fills the slot only if it measured response produced on the surface the brand's presence organized, at pre-conversion funnel stages. A figure that measured anything else can be placed in the slot, but it does not fill it. The slot looks occupied and is empty.

A figure now in circulation makes the violation concrete. A McKinsey & Company analysis of the gaming medium reports that a [10% increase in consumer focus moves with a 17% increase in consumer spend](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/gamings-next-growth-era-unlocking-the-value-of-attention). The figure is real and carefully produced. It is being cited as evidence that third-party brands convert audiences inside immersive environments — the bridge from the medium's attention to a brand's customer. It measures nothing of the kind, because the brand organized nothing it observed.

This piece uses that figure to show what the response slot will not admit: a borrowed figure can be placed in it but cannot fill it. The gate that enforces the line is what follows.

## A measurement records its context
A measurement records what was observed, where, and on whom. Those three facts are part of the measurement, not packaging around it. "17% more spend" means nothing until they are restored: spend by whom, on what, observed where. Strip them and the figure stops generalizing — it stops referring to anything. A statistic is a claim about the specific population, transaction, and environment it was drawn from. Outside them it is a numeral, not a finding.

## Relocation
Relocation is the movement of a valid metric out of its native context into the response slot. It runs on three axes. A metric is relocated on the funnel-stage axis when a figure drawn at one stage is cited as another — a monetization figure presented as pre-conversion response. It is relocated on the spender axis when one population's spend is cited as another population's. It is relocated on the environment axis when a figure produced on one surface is cited as produced on another.

Relocation on any one axis is sufficient. It usually travels with a second liberty: a correlation is upgraded to a cause, "moves with" restated as "drives." Relocation does not weaken the evidence. It removes the referent. A relocated statistic measures nothing in its new position, however rigorously it was produced in its old one.

## The relocation, worked through
A metric valid for one spender and one funnel, cited for another, measures nothing in the new position. The McKinsey figure holds the claim. In its native context the correlation measures the medium monetizing its own audience's attention. The medium is the organizing element of that context, not a third-party brand. The spender is the player buying inside the game. The funnel is the medium's monetization path, from a free player to a paying one, not a third-party brand's funnel. The figure measures how well the medium converts the attention it commands into the audience's spend on the medium itself.

The figure is then cited as evidence that a brand's presence inside the medium converts that audience into the brand's customers. The spender changes — from the player buying the game to the consumer buying an advertised product. The funnel changes — from the medium's monetization path to the brand's pre-conversion path. The figure was produced on the first spender and the first funnel and is entered for the second. Both native facts are stripped at once. What remains in the response slot is a number with no referent there. The correlation is real. It is real about something else.

## The same figure, caveat stripped
Relocation can strip a finding's caveat, not only dislocate it. The same figure shows how far the distortion can run. In its native analysis the correlation sits inside a caution: the medium commands attention it does not automatically convert to spend, and its most-engaged cohorts are not its largest spenders. The native point is that focus and spend move together, but too loosely to treat engagement as conversion — value is being left on the table.

Relocated into the response slot, the figure is read as proof that engagement converts. The caution is the first thing left behind. What the source offered as a warning — that engagement does not reliably become spend — is entered as a demonstration that it does. The figure does not change; its qualification disappears. That is relocation at its limit: the number is presented as endorsement of exactly what its native context cautioned against, with the hedge that would correct the reading stripped off in transit.

## The figure as ornament
Relocation's most damaging use is not as evidence but as ornament. After the figure is placed, a further claim is laid beside it — that the medium turns its players into a brand's customers — and that claim appears in no source at all. It is not a misreading of the figure. It is not in the figure. The number sits next to it lending the appearance of measurement, and proximity does the work inference cannot. A real figure beside an unsupported claim does not support it; it decorates it. The figure's rigor is borrowed to make the unmeasured part sound measured.

## Why the channel's standard does not catch this
The boundary test does not catch relocation, because the test adjudicates environments and relocation is a defect in citations. The test asks whether a surface produces direct behavioral evidence of consumer response. It catches a surface that produces none. It does not catch a number produced elsewhere and moved onto this surface in argument.

The output question a media planner runs — what brand-attributed response does this format produce, and on what basis is it comparable — names the right target but assumes the cited figure was measured on it. Relocation is a figure measured on a different target, presented as though measured on this one. The standard tests the surface. It does not test the citation. The citation is what this gate tests.

## The gate
A statistic qualifies as response evidence for an activation only if it passes one question. 

Was this number produced by observing brand-attributed consumer response, on the activation surface, at pre-conversion funnel stages? 

If it was produced in another funnel, on another spender, or on another surface, it is relocated, and relocation disqualifies it. The rigor of its native production does not transfer, because rigor attaches to the question a measurement answered, not to the number it returned. A figure carried across contexts carries its numeral and leaves its meaning behind.

##The finding
Immersive media's response evidence is admissible only from inside the environment that produced it. That is not an added rule. It is the channel's defining property restated as a constraint: the evidence is native, unified, and organized by the brand's presence, so it exists only on the surface that presence organizes, and a figure imported from a context the brand never organized cannot stand in for it.

The channel's evidence standard qualifies the environment. This gate qualifies the evidence cited for it. Both hold or the case fails, and they fail independently — an environment can pass the boundary test and still be defended by a figure that measured something else, somewhere else, on someone else. The qualification can be sound and the evidence offered for it empty, and from the outside the two are indistinguishable until the figure is traced back to the context that made it.
