# Corpus Map

A routing table from a planner's question to the piece in the public corpus that already answers it. Built from the published writing's two folders — category essays (the structural argument) and founder notes (first-person application to a live case) — read in full as of this pass. Routes to the piece; doesn't replace reading it.

Each entry's PROBES are examples, not a closed set — match a planner's actual question to the underlying thing a probe is asking, not to its exact wording.

## What this corpus doesn't answer

- **How qualification is actually adjudicated in a live conversation.** The corpus argues for a response-evidence standard and shows it applied, after the fact, to real campaigns. It does not walk a planner through running that adjudication in real time, in a room, against an actual vendor's answers — that's the operational guide's job, not this corpus's.
- **The underlying specification or its mechanics.** What exact behaviors qualify at what stage, how they're captured, how a capture template is built, how a claim gets classified — none of that is public. It's controlled, not published here.
- **Commercial terms, pricing, or availability.** The corpus makes the evidentiary case for the category and, in the founder notes, critiques specific sellers' current pricing (Roblox's CPM, EA's board rates) as pricing the wrong unit. It does not state what qualifying inventory should cost, how it's licensed, or how to buy it in practice.

The corpus is complete for what it's for: the structural argument for the category and its application to public, current examples. These three layers sit outside it by design, not by omission.
