An independent programmatic authority just sorted a large slice of gaming media into its native channel. It never mentioned immersive media. It landed outside the category boundary.

***

The Gaming Marketing Institute, which I founded, has published seven pieces making a central argument: gaming is not a channel. It is a collection of formats grouped under an audience label — otherwise known as the gaming bundle. Each format in the bundle belongs in the channel its response evidence and its buying motion already place it in.

Sort by what a format produces, not by who it reaches, and the bundle comes apart on its own. This week the bundle came apart a little more, in public, with no help from me.

Jounce Media — the supply-path authority programmatic buyers and sellers use to read the RTB market — named "desktop apps" a distinct premium ad environment for the first time, in its June 2026 report. Desktop apps are the gaming companion tools that run on-screen across titles like Minecraft and Fortnite.

Jounce found they now account for 11% of all web bid requests: a larger source of supply than any single web publisher, ahead of Yahoo, Raptive, and Mediavine. Overwolf is the category leader — 215 of the 241 desktop apps Jounce identified run on it.

That is a real classification event. It is worth reading closely, because of where it lands the inventory.

## The inventory is display
Every operative term in the report is programmatic-display vocabulary — bid requests, seller profiles, exchange profiles, viewability. The benchmark set is web publishers, not digital games and not immersive environments. Jounce compares Overwolf's monetization model to Raptive and Mediavine, which are web display ad-representation networks.

The format was not named a new channel. It was placed inside the open programmatic display chain, which is the channel it was always in. Gaming Is Not a Channel made this exact call: in-game display is display, hosted in a game environment, operating on display's terms. An independent authority just filed it there.

## The quality conversation is about viewability
This is the tell. Jounce's three buyer safeguards are all measures of the impression — identify the app cleanly, refresh only when half the ad's pixels are in view, and let MRC-accredited vendors confirm the ad was visible. Not one of them applied immersive media's advertising boundary test:

Does this environment produce direct behavioral evidence of consumer response at pre-conversion funnel stages?

Viewability is exposure. Immersive media's advertising boundary excludes exposure-based evidence standards. The market is grading this format on the exact proxy the boundary rules out — which is the strongest confirmation available that the format sits outside it. Immersive Media Explained already named Overwolf a proxy-based channel adjacent to immersive, for this reason. The report grades it on the proxy.

The label names the audience. The classification names the channel
Overwolf still markets a gaming audience — a reported 100+ million monthly gamers. Jounce's substance names something else: desktop-app display, benchmarked against web publishers. This is the pattern Gaming Budgets Don't Exist describes. The seller sells the audience. The accounting books the channel. The two come apart inside a single document, and the channel is display.

One precision, because it matters. Naming "desktop apps" a distinct environment is not the same as naming a new channel. It is a placement descriptor layered onto programmatic display — the way "gaming display" is an audience parameter, not a channel definition. The label is new. The channel underneath is display.

And one boundary, because the report does not cross it. The desktop apps run on-screen across immersive environments. Classifying the overlay surface as display says nothing about whether the games underneath qualify as immersive channels. An overlay adjacent to a qualifying environment is not inventory in that environment — Immersive Media Inventory, Explained draws that line. This report sorts the overlay. It leaves the environments alone.

## The bundle is dissolving on schedule
The report does two things at once.

It gives a display piece of the gaming bundle a home — a premium label, a benchmark, a line in an independent authority's books. That piece was always going to find its channel, because display has a channel, a buying motion, and a standard.

It also throws the asymmetry into relief. The immersive piece of the bundle — the one format that produces direct behavioral evidence of consumer response no other channel can produce — still has no standard to plan against and no line to book under. Desktop apps have a channel (display), a buying motion, and now a premium label. Immersive has none of the three.

That is the bundle dissolving on schedule. Each format returning to the channel it produces response evidence for. The piece that produces what nothing else can is the last one without a home. The seven pieces point at what would close that gap. This report is the market conceding the gap is there to close.
