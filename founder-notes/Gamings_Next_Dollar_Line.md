# Gaming's Next-Dollar Line
In August 2026 the IAB published The Business Case for Gaming. It is written for CMOs, and it does something most trade documents do not. It sets a pass/fail rule for its own recommendation. A CMO should approve a gaming plan when the business role is clear, the brand earns a role, the core risks are controlled, and the “evidence can support a next-dollar decision.” A CMO should pause when the audience fit is assumed, the brand adds nothing, or the measurement cannot answer whether the plan should scale.

That is the right rule. It is the rule finance already applies to every other line. The document deserves credit for writing it down.

Then the document applies the rule to gaming, and most of gaming does not pass.

## The test, applied to the table
IAB's document maps gaming to the funnel. Each business objective gets a set of formats and a set of example metrics. Read the metrics column on its own.

For awareness: impressions, viewable impressions, in-view rate, time in view, reach, frequency, recall.

For consideration: engagement rate, completion, interactions, clicks, brand lift, site visits.

For brand equity: preference, favorability, share of conversation, earned media, lift studies, qualitative signals.

Every one of these is a delivery metric or a reconstruction. Impressions and in-view rate say the ad was there. Completion and interactions say someone did something inside the ad unit. Recall, lift, preference, and favorability are survey instruments, bought separately, fielded after the buy, and reported in a currency the buy does not share. None of them says whether the brand’s presence moved anyone at the stage the brief was paying for.

The document’s own CMO framework has a measurement row that asks which evidence will determine whether the plan scales, changes, or stops. The funnel table does not answer it for any brand objective. It answers it for conversion, where installs, redemptions, and cost per action exist, and for nothing above conversion.

By the document’s own pause condition, that is a pause.

This is not a flaw in the document. It is an accurate picture. The formats in the table are bought the way every brand channel is bought: with the question open, and with the answer purchased later from someone else.

## What the next dollar actually requires
Strip the rule to what it needs. A next-dollar decision requires four things.

The evidence is about this buy, not about the channel in aggregate.

The evidence is in the currency the brief is paying for. For a brand brief, that is a pre-conversion stage.

The evidence arrives in time to shape the next allocation.

The evidence is attributed to the brand’s presence, not to the environment or the audience.

These four conditions are not new. They are the standard performance channels established. Search and social pass the first three cleanly: per placement, in conversion, in near real time. They pass the fourth by convention. Attribution is modeled after the fact, and the industry has agreed to accept the model while arguing about it.

That standard is why performance lines scale. Each buy earns the next on its own record. Nobody has to make a case for the channel. The last buy made it.

Brand spend has never had a line that meets the standard, because the only instruments that meet it observe conversion. Everything above conversion is measured by a second purchase. A lift study is a survey about recall. A marketing mix model is a coefficient on aggregate spend. A geo-holdout observes sales, which is not what the brand brief was buying. The brand pays for the media, pays again for the instrument, waits for the result, and still cannot say what the first payment did at the stage it targeted.

Brand lines have grown without that standard. Television, out-of-home, and online video scaled on a currency: a standard unit, a count every buyer accepts, and an agreement to treat delivery as the value bought. The currency does not say what any buy did. It makes buys comparable, so the next dollar can follow habit and commitment instead of a record. Most of the gaming bundle can be bought the same way, and it should be.

One line in gaming cannot. Without a currency and without evidence, it grows the way sponsorship grew: outside the media plan, sized by commitment, and capped at what leadership can defend. It has to earn the next dollar the other way.

## The one line that passes
There is one line in gaming that can meet the standard at a brand stage. It is the line the IAB document files under “custom integrations” and “branded worlds,” measures with custom KPIs and lift studies, and treats as the expensive end of the gaming media bundle.

That filing misses what the category can do. In an immersive environment, the brand’s presence is not an ad unit placed near play. It is a role inside the environment: a place, an object, a thing people can use or choose or walk past. The environment records what people do with it. And because the environment is built, not bought, the same role can exist in a non-branded form for a control group in the same session, at the same stage, in the same world.

When that is the design, the delivery and the evidence are the same event. The buy produces a response rate to the brand’s presence, against a control, at the stage the brief targets, within the window of the buy. The evidence is about this buy, in the brief’s currency, on time, and attributed by a control built into the purchase rather than modeled afterwards.

That meets all four conditions. On the fourth it is stronger than most performance lines, because the control is inside the buy.

However, the line is not performance media. The objective is pre-conversion, and no conversion claim is made. It is the performance evidence standard applied to a brand objective. It is the only brand buy that does not require a second purchase to find out what it did.

That makes it gaming’s next-dollar line. The possessive is doing exact work. Of everything the IAB calls gaming, one line meets the IAB’s own test. The rest is reach. Reach is a legitimate thing to buy. It should be priced like reach, bought like reach, and never described as growth.

It also makes it something larger than a gaming story. Brand spend, meaning the money allocated to objectives above conversion, has never had a line that earns the next dollar on its own record. Search has one. Retail media has one. Brand spend has had strategic commitment and a reconstruction bill. This is the first next-dollar line brand spend has ever had. That it currently lives in gaming is a fact about where the environments are built today, not about what the line is.

Two limits
The claim is narrow, and it should stay narrow.

First, the evidence closes the question for the buy, not for the channel. It does not say immersive works. It says this buy did or did not move people at this stage. That is how performance evidence behaves. It is also why performance evidence scales: each buy stands on its own record, and the record accumulates.

Second, pre-conversion response is stronger than recall because it is observed and attributed. It is not a sale. Relating it to what happens downstream is a calibration problem, solved over time with a record. It is not the entry claim. The moment the claim drifts toward outcomes, every open-question channel gets to ask the same question back, and the distinction collapses.

A third note belongs here. The standard is defined and testable. It has not yet been met at scale, because the planning discipline that would demand it does not yet exist inside most agencies. A brief that specifies a control, a response window, and a stage, and that pays the builder against the evidence, is the mechanism. The IAB’s own planner companion asks for a measurement rule and a scale threshold in every gaming brief. For one line, that rule can be written before the buy and answered by the buy.

## What the document should say next time
The IAB document closes its funnel section by saying gaming has earned a place in the marketing plan. That is a claim about the entire gaming media bundle, and the bundle cannot support it by the document’s own rule.

The defensible version is smaller and more useful. One line in gaming can show what it did at the stage a brand brief pays for, before anyone has to model it. That line should be adjudicated on every brand brief, admitted when the brief can carry the evidence, and bought with the question closed. Everything else in the bundle is reach, and reach should be bought as reach.

That is a business case a CMO can approve. It is one a CFO can read. And it is not, in the end, a case for gaming. It is the first time brand spend has had a line that can make its own case.
