Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6) ran the largest gaming audience-location study in advertising history, in public, for free. The result settles where the gaming audience actually lives.


When Rockstar Games posted the first trailer for GTA 6, it drew more than 200 million views — the second-most-watched gaming trailer in history. The second trailer set a new entertainment-industry record for launch-day views, a record that stood until Spider-Man: Brand New Day surpassed it this year. A title still more than a year from release produced two of the largest video events gaming has ever recorded.

The Gaming Marketing Institute, which I founded, published a claim in The Truth About Gaming Audiences — Part 1: The Place, Not the Person: gaming audiences are reachable today, at scale, on every conventional channel, and the only thing that was ever scarce is the context — the place, not the person.

Testing that claim with behavior, not structure, requires a signal no media plan can manufacture. It doesn't require zero spend behind the content — nobody outside Take-Two (Rockstar's owner) knows what supported those trailers, and this test doesn't need to know either. What it requires is a response layer that can't be bought at any price: independent creators, unprompted and unpaid, making reaction videos, breakdowns, and clip compilations, and choosing on their own where to post them. No line item produces that. It either happens, at the audience's own initiative, or it doesn't.

A response layer that size requires an event large enough to produce it unprompted. Exactly one franchise in this category has the recognition to produce one — the Elvis of the medium, a property whose awareness is saturated before a dollar of media runs. That franchise has now run the experiment twice, and it is running the full campaign version through November 19th.

## The result is public
Neither of those numbers is the evidence this piece needs. A publisher's own channel, and whatever promotional support sat behind it, is an unknown this argument doesn't have to resolve. The evidence sits in the layer around the trailers: reaction videos, frame-by-frame breakdowns, and clip compilations, made by creators Rockstar didn't hire and posted on platforms Rockstar didn't book, generating billions of additional impressions across TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and X.

Read that list of venues again. TikTok. YouTube Shorts. Reels. X. Every one is conventional inventory — open, commodity-priced, interest-targetable, bought by every advertiser in every category every day. Nobody commissions a reaction video, and nobody pays a stranger to post a breakdown. The most gaming-obsessed population on earth produced billions of impressions with no media plan behind a single one of them, and it produced them on the open internet — not inside specialist gaming media.

No media plan produced that layer. The audience located itself, and in choosing where to react, it published its own location data at a sample size no study could fund. If this audience's attention lived behind specialist gaming media (as endemic actors assert), accessible only at a premium — the reaction would have concentrated there. Instead, it concentrated where brands are already buying media at scale.

One line has to be drawn precisely here, because the argument's own evidence standard requires it. Unpaid reaction volume is not a paid campaign, and this event does not prove that conventional targeting reaches this audience. That proof doesn't need an event; it is established by the targeting products themselves, sold as standard inventory and cleared daily. 

What GTA 6 supplies is the thing a structural claim can never generate on its own: a demonstration, at maximum possible scale, that the population those products sell shows up — unpaid, uncommissioned — exactly where the products say it is. The structure was already proven. The behavior made it visible.

## The evidence is the audience, not the media buy
There is a tempting version of this argument, and it should be refused. The tempting version reads Rockstar's reported nine-figure ad budget, notes the mass channels in it, and concludes: even Rockstar doesn't need gaming media to reach gamers.

That version fails three ways. Rockstar is an endemic advertiser — its advertising is gaming content, so its channel choices prove nothing about what a non-endemic brand needs. The actual mix is undisclosed, and Take-Two has all but confirmed heavy creator-native spend, so the claim "Rockstar skipped gaming media" is one screenshot away from public falsification. And GTA 6 carries a Mature rating, which bars it from much of the youth-skewing inventory the gaming bundle sells — meaning any absence of gaming media in its plan is partly compelled, not chosen, and a revealed preference requires a choice.

The spend is contaminated evidence. The reaction is not. It does not depend on a single planning decision Rockstar made, on a single line of the mix, or on what is disclosed later. Whatever Rockstar buys between now and November, the record of what unpaid creators made — and where they chose to post it — already exists.

Rockstar's history is admissible only as color. GTA V ran a twenty-two-month campaign in 2011–2013 on television, billboards, print magazine covers, and radio — the apex endemic marketer, treating mass channels as sufficient for the biggest launch in the medium's history. That supports the finding. It is not the finding.

## What this settles, and what it doesn't
This settles the reach question. The person was never scarce, never hiding, and never behind a paywall of specialist access. When the biggest event in gaming happened, the audience was findable by anyone with a feed — because it was in the feed.

It does not say gaming media is worthless. Non-interruptive endemic formats genuinely fit this cohort, and plans built on that fit are legitimate — the audience series grants that ground in full. And it says nothing against the one context Part 1 named as genuinely scarce: brand presence inside the world itself, where attention demonstrably occurs and where nothing has historically been buyable.

In fact, GTA 6 sharpens that boundary rather than just confirming it. Every conventional channel the reaction played out on — TikTok, YouTube, Reels, X — is exactly as buyable by a shampoo brand as it was by Rockstar; nothing about GTA 6 gave Rockstar preferential access to that inventory. But GTA 6 itself is not for sale at any price. 

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick has ruled out real-life brands in the game outright, telling The Game Business that interstitial advertising in a title people paid $70 or $80 for "would seem unfair." A non-endemic brand can match Rockstar dollar-for-dollar on every conventional channel in this campaign. It cannot buy a single billboard inside Vice City. Reach is solved everywhere the audience scrolls; the one thing left scarce is the world itself, and its owner has taken it off the market.

The people were never the scarce thing. GTA 6 just showed you where they were the whole time.
