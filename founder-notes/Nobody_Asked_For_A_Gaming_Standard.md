A note on IAB UK's gaming-measurement roundtable — and the one unit the standard it's reaching for cannot produce.

A measurement standard for gaming media is being built right now, and the first thing I noticed was who is building it. The parties asking for it sell the inventory it would price. No brand asked them to. That composition is the tell, and it points at something the effort cannot fix: the unit everyone is converging on cannot be built — not because the methodology is immature, but because one member of what it would count has no container to count.

A countable unit needs a container that exists before the advertiser arrives. Immersive brand presence is the opposite case: the advertiser builds it, so nothing pre-exists it to count. That does not leave immersive unmeasurable. It leaves it without a currency, which is not the same as without a standard. A standard is a criterion, and a criterion holds constant while the object it admits varies. 

A currency requires a container shared across advertisers, and in immersive the activation is unique to the brand. The only shared container is the venue, so a currency there prices the venue, not the brand. Immersive can have the criterion and never the currency. The unit under construction is reaching for the currency, which is the reason it cannot be built.

## The room
The exercise is already under way, and the room assembled to run it gives the finding away before a single unit is proposed. In June 2026, IAB UK convened a roundtable on how gaming should be measured and published it through The Drum. Five participants: Unity, Dubit, Overwolf, December19 | B Corp™, and Goodstuff. Four sell gaming inventory or represent platforms that do. One is an agency. No brand was in the room, and a measurement standard exists to serve the brand.

The composition determines the output. A standard gives the buyer a basis to compare spend across allocation decisions, so it is a demand-side instrument by function. Convened without the demand side, the exercise becomes a debate about which seller-reported number becomes the unit. That is what the transcript records. Unity proposes a common identity structure Unity built. Dubit proposes platform-reported visitors, time engaged, and repeat visits. Overwolf proposes attention units supplied by a measurement vendor. Every unit proposed is authored by a party with inventory in the outcome.

The diagnosis inside the room is nonetheless correct, and it is worth quoting because it concedes the case. Dubit's chief commercial officer states plainly that "The impression is the least interesting thing about gaming," and describes brands on Roblox and Fortnite as building places people choose to spend time rather than buying attention. That description is accurate. It is a description of a brand constructing the surface it occupies. The observation is right, and the unit proposed to price it — visitor counts — measures the venue's traffic, not the brand's effect. That gap is the whole of what follows.

## Every countable unit rests on the same fact
A countable advertising unit exists because the container pre-exists the advertiser. A 300x250 exists before any brand fills it, persists after that brand's flight ends, and receives the next advertiser unchanged. A thirty-second pre-roll slot is the same. A fourteen-by-forty-eight bulletin is the same. The publisher or media owner builds the container; the advertiser supplies what goes in it. The impression counts the container's rendering, not the brand's presence.

That split is the precondition for every currency in media. Impressions, views, and time-in-view are all counts of a container doing what it was built to do. They are comparable across advertisers because the container is identical across advertisers, and they are comparable across quarters because the container is still there next quarter.

Immersive media inverts the split. A branded world, an in-world integration, a branded game — remove the brand and the surface is gone or fundamentally changed, because the brand constructed it. Nothing was waiting to be filled. In immersive, the advertiser constructs the format.

A format is a container that pre-exists its occupant, so immersive brand presence has no format. This is not a shortfall in the taxonomy. It is a structural property of inventory the advertiser authors, and it holds however the taxonomy is written.

## The pattern across media
The unit exists exactly where the container pre-exists the advertiser, and nowhere else. Immersive is not an exception to media's rule. It is the one category composed entirely of the case every other channel already handles by negotiation.

Out-of-home makes the point from inside. It is the channel most often cited as proof that placed, un-served inventory can be counted, and it already contains inventory it cannot count. Bulletins, posters, and transit carry the currency. Spectaculars, wraps, and custom builds do not — they are advertiser-authored, priced by negotiation, and sit outside the standard. 

Product placement in film and television is the same shape at full extension: no format, no currency, negotiated terms, and recall studies in place of a unit. Immersive is made entirely of spectaculars.

## Estimation does not rescue it
The strongest reply is that a delivery event was never required. Out-of-home has no delivery decision and prices on impressions anyway, because Geopath estimates them from traffic counts, panel data, and visibility modeling. The estimate is accredited and transacted. Immersive could do the same, and vendors already produce viewport-based impression figures for in-game placements.

The reply fails on a precondition rather than on principle. An estimation methodology requires a stable, recurring inventory universe to be normative over. A bulletin at a fixed location has a measurable flow past a measurable face, the same face is there next quarter for the next advertiser, and a panel can be built and validated against it. Immersive inventory is authored per activation, exists for a flight, and may never recur. There is no panel to build for a population of one.

The estimate also has an author, and the author's standing is the whole question. Out-of-home installed an independent estimator precisely so the seller would not author its own currency. In immersive, the only parties positioned to author the estimate are the ones selling the inventory — which returns the argument to the room this piece opened on.

## Grant the bundle
Nothing in the argument so far has depended on gaming being more than one channel, and that is deliberate. There is a stronger objection to a single gaming standard, and the case does not need it. 

Gaming is an audience interest spanning display, video, creator, sponsorship, experiential, and immersive, and a currency denominates one channel rather than six. That objection is true, it is made elsewhere, and it persuades only a reader who already accepts the bundle's dissolution.

So concede it. Grant that gaming is a single channel with a single inventory pool. The single-unit hypothesis still fails, and it fails on the one member the argument has already isolated: immersive.

## The framework named it and counted anyway
That single-channel treatment has already been attempted once, and the attempt recorded its own failure. The IAB's June 2025 Gaming Measurement Framework sorted gaming formats into four metrics matrices. Three are display, video, and audio. The fourth is headed CUSTOM, and its columns are branded world, integrations, sponsorship, playables, livestream/social, skins/logos, branded games, and digital goods/UGC.

Those are not formats. They are a retrospective list of things brands have built, placed in the column position a format taxonomy occupies. Custom means built to order, and built to order means no container was waiting. The header states the condition this piece describes.

Underneath that header the framework supplies impressions, impression count, CPM, viewability, in-view rate, unique reach, and ad frequency. The framework named the unboundedness in the column header and specified the container-rendering metrics beneath it regardless. The contradiction is on one page — printed by the standards body, not inferred by me.

## No replacement supplies a currency
The room did not only propose the impression. Asked what to install instead, it offered unique visitors, time engaged, repeat visits, a re-contactable audience, attention time, attention units, brand lift, sessions played, and average playtime. If any of these were a currency for immersive, the standard would be buildable after all.

None is, and the reason is the container asymmetry restated rather than a fresh objection. A currency prices a container shared across advertisers, and the immersive activation is not shared, because the brand built it. So every native count in the list falls to one side of a single line: it measures a shared container that is not the brand's, or it measures the brand's and is shared with no one. 

Platform-reported visitors, sessions, and playtime count the venue the brand entered — the game, the world, the platform. That container pre-exists the brand and recurs, so its count is comparable across advertisers, but it is the venue's count and not the brand's; remove the brand and it barely moves. 

A branded world's own visitor figure is the opposite case: it is genuinely the brand's, because the brand built the world — but the brand built that world, so the figure is unique to it and comparable to no other advertiser's. 

The rest are not native counts at all: attention units are modeled onto venue traffic, brand lift is a survey run beside it, and neither reads a surface the inventory produced. The metric that is about the brand is never the metric identical across advertisers, because the only surface identical across advertisers is the one the brand did not build.

The strongest version of the venue's claim deserves granting in full. Overwolf's brand partnerships director argues that entry into a game is deliberate, that nobody opens a game by accident, and that this produces a focused environment conventional measurement ignores. All of it holds — and all of it belongs to the venue. A non-branded equivalent in the same position collects the same focused attention, which is why the focus is the operator's asset and not the brand's evidence.

So the debate is not between the impression and something better. It is over which venue count gets standardized — and you are letting the parties who own the venues pick it.

## What is bounded
The format is unbounded. The response is not.

A brand can build a vehicle, a quest, a wearable, a storefront, or an entire world. What a consumer can do toward that presence has four shapes: a discretionary action whose object is the brand's presence; interaction sustained or repeated beyond what the environment compels; a choice favouring the brand while a real alternative was present; and a forward commitment short of the transaction itself. Those four are the same for a soda brand in a racing title and a bank in a social world.

That is the asymmetry a standard can be built on. A standard is a criterion; a currency is an instance. A criterion can be constant while the object it admits varies, because the criterion tests what the object does rather than what it is. A currency requires the object to be identical for every advertiser, which is exactly what advertiser-authored inventory cannot supply. Immersive can have the criterion and never the currency, and confusing the two is what makes the missing unit look like a missing standard.

The surface is unbounded by format and bounded twice over in practice. It is bounded by the environment's grammar — a brand can be anything the environment can render and instrument, which is wide and is not infinite. And it is bounded by the specification, written before the build. The demand side authors that specification: the brand and its planning function, not the operator and not a consensus body. Authorship of the container and authority over its specification are one fact. Where the advertiser builds the unit, the advertiser defines what counts as its performance, because there is nothing to define until something is built.

This is why no external body can pre-specify the unit. A standards body defines a viewable impression in advance because the impression exists in advance. It cannot define a branded quest in advance, because no branded quest exists until a brand builds one.

## Withdrawing the concession
Return the bundle to its actual state and the last piece falls into place. The IAB framework slotted display, video, and audio cleanly into three matrices, and the clean slotting is the proof. A format cannot be sorted into a channel unless a standard is already waiting there to receive it. The framework's success on three matrices is the evidence for the fourth matrix's failure.

That accounts for the silence this piece is named after. Nobody is asking for a new display standard, because display has one. Nobody is asking for a new video standard, or a new audio standard, or a new sponsorship standard. The formats in the gaming bundle that belong to established channels are already served on those channels' terms, priced in those channels' units, and booked on those channels' lines. The standard under construction would duplicate what three of its four members already have and stay unbuildable for the fourth.

## The finding
The gaming measurement standard is a supply-side project. It was proposed without buyers, it would price a bundle that is not a channel, and it would denominate that bundle in a unit one member structurally cannot produce, because in immersive the advertiser builds the container and there is no container-rendering to count.

The standard brands actually need is the one nobody in that room proposed, and it is a criterion rather than a unit. Does this environment produce direct behavioral evidence of consumer response to the brand's presence, at pre-conversion funnel stages, captured by the environment's native instrumentation? That question admits the environments that observe a consumer responding to a brand and excludes the surfaces that only carry exposure, and it holds constant across advertisers while the surfaces it admits vary completely.

Nobody asked for a gaming standard, and the absence of the ask is the finding. Brands aren't asking for a new unit for display or video, because they hold both already. They aren't asking for one for immersive, because no one has told them there is a category to ask about. The standard is buildable. It just isn't the one in that room.
