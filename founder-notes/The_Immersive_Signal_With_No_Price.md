# The Immersive Signal With No Price

A note on why clicks (still) rule — and what that proves about what immersive media is missing.
***

I established the immersive media standard because I keep watching the right response evidence get produced and then set aside. The following highlights the clearest case I've seen all year. A studio did the hard thing, captured exactly the signal the channel is defined by, and still put a click-through rate in the headline. That choice is not a mistake. It's the channel telling you what it can and cannot count.

I'm a huge supporter of what Wicked Saints Studios has built with World Reborn. The experience is built around real-world quests rather than in-game ad inventory, and the quests instrument what players actually do inside the world.

When a brand sponsored the Starlight quest, players were offered a pre-written note to a hospitalized child or the option to write their own, and the studio reports that fifty-four percent wrote their own within the first ten minutes. That figure is the qualifying signal.

It is a direct behavioral response, captured by the environment's own instrumentation, observed in one place, attributable to the brand's presence because the brand organized the surface the response happened on. Most of the category cannot produce that. Wicked Saints built an environment that does.

And then it led with the click. The number the studio puts forward is a thirty-one percent click-through rate across its launch partners, set against Roblox's sub-one-percent rate. That's because a click-through has a market and the note-choice does not. A planner can price a click.

The same planner has no slot for "brand-attributed response captured in place," no benchmark to read it against, no line to book it under. The note-choice benchmarks against nothing, so it reports as color, and the click reports as the result — even though the click is the weaker evidence and the studio produced the stronger one.

Precedence here is not assigned by evidentiary strength. It's assigned by comparability. A click is engagement with the prompt — not evidence of a funnel state. In other words, it is a proxy. But it is a proxy with a comparison set, and the in-place response is a finding with none. A proxy the market can read beats a signal the market cannot. That is the whole of it. The better evidence loses precedence because no standard exists to price it, and the worse evidence wins because the market was built around it.

Sounds pretty conclusive, right?

Clicks win. Get over it.

Here's the issue. A studio that built native response capture describes its output in the one metric that capture was supposed to make unnecessary. You make do with a proxy signal when direct behavioral evidence of brand-attributed response is not available.

Wicked Saints didn't make do. The evidence was available, captured, and reported — and the click still led.

Immersive media can produce that qualifying response evidence. Yet, its media environments are still pricing themselves in display's response currency.

None of this turns on whether the studio's numbers are right. The thirty-one percent could be exact or generous and the argument is unchanged. What's observable is the reporting choice — an operator that produces qualifying evidence foregrounded a click and benchmarked it against a click. The choice is the data here, not the rate.

This is an indictment of a missing channel standard, not the studio. Here the operator is strong, purpose-built, doing the harder and better-instrumented thing, and the click still leads. That is the cleanest proof available that the constraint is the missing standard, not the medium.

The category can't yet count its own best evidence.

The channel produces a class of evidence nothing else does — direct behavioral response to a brand's presence, at pre-conversion stages, captured natively, observed in one place. World Reborn produces it.

What the channel lacks is the thing that would let a buyer rank it: a definition of the unit, a comparison set, a price, a line in the plan. Absent that, even the operators producing the signal will keep leading with the proxy, because the proxy is the only thing a buyer can act on. The proxy's precedence is not a verdict on the evidence. It's a verdict on the market.

Establish in-place response as a standard and the order inverts back to where it belongs. The note-choice gets a comparison set, a price, and a line, and it leads — because it is the evidence the brief actually needs. The click reverts to what it always was, a proxy, useful at the edges and never the headline. The standard doesn't make the click worse. It makes the better signal countable — and once the better signal can be counted, no operator has to bury it to be understood.

That's the finding, and it's why the work is a standard and not a metric. The channel does not have an evidence problem. Wicked Saints proves the evidence is there, in place, today. The channel has a counting problem. It cannot yet price the best thing it produces, so the next-best thing it borrows leads. Fixing that is not a measurement upgrade. It's the difference between a channel and a surface that keeps getting sold in another channel's units.
