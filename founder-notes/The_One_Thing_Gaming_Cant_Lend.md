# The One Thing Gaming Can't Lend

Two gaming advertising vendors launched products in the same week of September, and each crossed the in-game environment boundary. Livewire carried creative in: the same assets marketers already use for social, dropped into in-game billboards and interstitials without a change. iion carried mechanics out: playables and mini-games built for games, deployed to the open web and social with the same analytics attached. 

Both launches use the environment as leverage for a narrative, and both work by lending out something the environment has. What they lend is real. What they leave behind is the only property that would make the environment a channel.

## The boundary the bundle names
Gaming's stated advertising boundary is play. Interactivity is the characteristic every positioning deck leads with: lean-in against lean-back, participation against exposure, a medium the consumer acts inside rather than watches. Holding-company playable programs are branded as interactive media on that basis. In other words, the mechanic is presented as the thing a buyer can only get in gaming.

iion's launch removes that claim, and it removes it from the inside. The company whose product is the mechanic has built a studio to deploy the mechanic across its gaming ecosystem, premium open web, and social channels, and its stated ambition is to take play beyond gaming. A boundary is something the buyer can only get on one side of. A property that ships to a feed was never a boundary. It was the environment's native creative idiom, the way motion is video's and sound is audio's, and no channel has ever been cut by a creative idiom.

The record confirms it. A playable produces engagement rate, time in unit, and completion. That is interaction with the ad, and the IAB's own gaming framework files it as a display metric, footnoted for rich media. The playable records interaction in-game. It records the same interaction on the web. The export did not make the record ordinary. It revealed that the record always was.

## Livewire closes the retort
What about venue claims? The same creative works harder in a game because the player is present by choice and not scrolling past, right? That is the strongest version of the channel claim, and Livewire's launch states it as a product. Unchanged creative, same workflow as social, placed in billboards and interstitials inside premium environments, sold on engagement the feed can't match.

Grant the claim in full. Suppose the lift is durable and larger than clutter and novelty explain. What has been shown is that the same display unit earns more attention in a game than in a feed. Attention is a metric built to travel; it exists so a unit in a game, a pre-roll, and a street panel can be compared on one scale. A metric engineered as a common currency cannot mark a boundary, because the moment it distinguishes one channel it stops working as a currency. A durable attention advantage prices a venue. Premium OOH earns more than a bus shelter. Yet, OOH is not two channels. In-game display earns a premium and it is display.

Livewire's own build says as much. The creative is unchanged, so nothing about the unit needs to know it is in a game. The formats are billboards and interstitials, rows the IAB framework already routes to display standards. The audience is bought through a gameplay-behavior ID that reaches players across mobile, console, PC, and TV, which makes the context an audience label that follows the person out of the venue. The venue premium may well be real, and every part of the product treats it as a display premium.

## Why a property can be lent
A property distinguishes a channel when it produces evidence the buyer can obtain nowhere else. That is the test. Social and retail media earned their lines because the seller observed what the consumer did after the exposure, attributed it, and held the record exclusively. The formats were banners. The record was new.

Attention and mechanics fail the test for the same reason, and their portability is the symptom. A property that can be lent without friction is one whose evidence the buyer already gets everywhere. Attention describes the exposure. Interaction describes the unit. Both records exist in every exposure channel, which is why both can be carried across the boundary and land intact. The lending did not disqualify them. It is proof of disqualification.

Nor can the billboard be rescued by measuring it differently. A gaming attention score is an attention score with a label. A record that describes the exposure under a new name is the same record. The only way a surface earns a boundary is by recording something different in kind, and a billboard cannot record something different in kind because nothing about it organizes the world around the brand. There is nothing for the environment to attribute.

## What the environment keeps
The one property neither launch carried across is the environment's native observation of what the player did after the brand's presence was introduced. For instance, a live-service game title records activation, engagement, retention, and spend because its economy cannot run otherwise. That grade of telemetry is running under every Livewire billboard and every iion mini-game. It is the one thing that can't be relocated. 

In other words, neither product reads it, and neither could because both sit outside immersive media's channel boundary. Immersive's response record lives in the environment. It requires the brand's presence to be organized into the world rather than displayed inside it, and a non-branded twin in the same role is specified so the comparison exists. The property cannot be lent because it is not a feature of a unit or a placement. It is a consequence of what was built.

So the asymmetry is complete. Everything the two launches lean on is something the environment lends: attention, presence by choice, a mechanic the player leans into. Every one of those travels, and every one lands in the evidence class it came from. The one thing the environment keeps is the one that makes it a channel.
