# Interactive Is Not A Channel

Holding companies have moved playables into scaled programs this year, with publisher partnerships attached and case studies to follow, and the programs are presented as interactive media, a category of its own. The first engagement rates will circulate as evidence that gaming converts. The numbers will be real. They will also come from a class that already has a home.

Every gaming ad format belongs to one of three evidence classes, and the class is set by what the surface records, not by what the consumer can do with the ad. A format that shows the consumer something records an exposure. A format the consumer can act on records an interaction with the ad. A surface inside an environment the consumer chose, organized by the brand's presence, records what the consumer did toward the brand. Those are three different records. They are not three grades of the same one.

## Grant every class its ground
In-game display is good display. It reaches a cohort that avoids conventional interruption, in a context with less fatigue and more attention, and its viewability clears the same accredited standard display clears everywhere. A planner who buys it on those terms is planning well. 

Playables are the best rich media in years. A consumer who spends forty seconds inside a branded mini-game has given the unit more than any banner earns, and the engagement rates prove it. The effectiveness is real, and it belongs to the unit. A format can outperform its open-web equivalent by a wide margin and the concession costs nothing, because outperformance is a claim about how the unit did. The question here is what the unit recorded.

## What each class records
The exposure class records that the unit was seen. Viewability, completion, attention scores, and modeled lift all describe the exposure or estimate what followed it. The brand's effect is reconstructed downstream, never observed at the surface. That is display's evidence class, and display has built the largest budget lines in media on it. The class is complete. It has a unit, a price, a comparison set, and a line in every plan.

The unit-interaction class records that the consumer did something to the ad. Engagement rate, time in unit, completion, click-out. The record is richer than an exposure, and it is still a record about the ad. The number cannot separate response to the brand from response to a free thirty-second game. It measures the game. There is no non-branded twin to read against. A playable for a pizza chain cannot be compared to the same playable without the pizza chain, because without the pizza chain there is no playable.

The brand-attributed response class records that the consumer did something toward the brand's presence, inside an environment they chose to be in, against a non-branded equivalent in the same role. The comparison against the twin is the record. A branded storefront players walk to when a plain storefront is available; a branded item players equip when an equivalent unbranded item is available; a queue players join for a branded drop with nothing compelling them there. Each is a behavior the brand's presence organized, read at a named pre-conversion stage, captured by the environment's own instrumentation. No other class produces it, because no other class has an environment to observe it in.

## Interactivity does not move a format across
The mistake is to read these classes as a ladder, with interactive one rung below immersive. Interactivity changes the rate inside a class. It does not change the class. A reward attached to a video changes the completion rate and leaves a completion exactly what it was. A game attached to a banner changes the engagement rate and leaves an engagement exactly what it was. Both formats got better at producing their native record. Neither produced a different one.

That is why a playable's number cannot climb. The unit travels across gaming, social, and the open web, and the game is inside it. In other words, it has no environment. Without an environment there is no organizing presence, no native instrumentation, and no twin. The format is delivered wherever inventory exists, and its record is whatever the ad captured about itself.

## The third column refuses to list formats
Branded worlds, integrations, and branded games are the environments where the third class can be produced. Being one of them puts nothing in the third class. Capable is not admitted. The same branded world instrumented for brand-attributed response is inventory. The same world uninstrumented is décor in a qualifying location, and it records what décor records: traffic through the venue and impressions on the walls.

So the third column has no format list, and that is the column's defining row. An activation sits there because it ran the comparison, not because of the kind of build it was. That's because a format list would let the build inherit the class, and inheritance is exactly how exposure numbers get carried into a column they never came from.

## One world, three records
A qualifying environment can hold all three classes at once. A Roblox experience can run a display billboard, a rewarded video slot, and an instrumented brand surface in the same world, in front of the same player, in the same minute. The environment and the surface are two different levels, and nothing passes between them in either direction. The environment decides what class is possible. The surface decides what class is recorded.

The container does not leak downward. A billboard inside a qualifying experience records that a wall was in view. It does not become brand-attributed response because of where it hangs, for the same reason a branded world does not become brand-attributed response because of what it is. Capacity is never inherited by the unit. The billboard is display, adjudicated on viewability, exactly as it is sold, and the environment's ability to record more is irrelevant to a unit that records less.

The units do not leak upward either. Running billboards and rewarded video inside the experience demotes nothing beside them. An activation can contain several surfaces, and each is its own run: the billboard reports exposure, the video reports completion, the branded item chosen over an unbranded twin reports response. Three channels share one venue and one consumer, and only one of them is native to it. Sharing a venue transfers nothing about evidence. Channel membership is set by what a surface records, not by where it sits.

The billboard shows how close the capacity sits to the placement. The environment can observe players walking toward a wall, lingering at it, and returning, and a plain billboard in the same spot would serve as the twin. 

Nobody (currently) runs that comparison. The unit is sold and priced as a viewable impression, but could qualify for another evidence class if designed accordingly. The capacity is present, the product records exposure, and the price follows the product.

## The check to run on a cited number
Two questions sort any figure a deck presents. Which surface produced it? What was the non-branded twin?

A number with no twin came from the first or second class. It may be an excellent number. It says the unit performed well, and a planner should weigh that inside the channel the unit belongs to. That evidence presented in the third column, as evidence the brand's presence moved anyone, is a relocation error.

The engagement rate that circulates next quarter belongs in the second class. That is the finding. The number is real. Read its row correctly.
