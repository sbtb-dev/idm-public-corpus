A second question belongs in the same meeting as the first, whether or not the first got answered. Is EA Buying the Billboards It Sells? asked whether Electronic Arts (EA) points its own board at its own titles. This one removes the ownership excuse: does EA buy anyone's — a board it doesn't own, on a platform it doesn't run, inside the football audience it says it wants?

That audience sits on Roblox. NFL Universe Football, Super NFL Tycoon, and FIFA Super Soccer are among the platform's largest football experiences, drawing the young, self-selected audience EA's own franchise-aging anxiety is about. 

Whether EA buys presence in that population — not whether it should build a rival world there — is a question a brand can put to EA directly, before paying a premium built on the assumption that gaming inventory reaches this audience.

## Same Product, Same Distribution Model
EA's own immersive billboard inventory doesn't sit at one fixed stadium. Frostbite's ad server rotates it across EA's portfolio of games. Roblox runs the identical mechanism at platform scale: Immersive Ads are image and video boards an ad server distributes across every experience that enables them. 

That is the literal, format-matched equivalent of EA's own stadium signage, scoreboards, and broadcast overlays — same media type, same rotation logic, same buying motion. The question is not whether EA should build a Roblox world. It is whether EA buys space in a rotation that already reaches its audience.

## Access Is Solved, At Every Tier
A separate class of Roblox inventory sits alongside the board: bespoke, built-for-hire integrations. The National Football League (NFL) has held a Roblox presence since 2022, trusting Gamefam with Super NFL Tycoon and Voldex with NFL Universe Football. HUGO BOSS integrated its NFL apparel line into Super NFL Tycoon, and Intuit sponsored the NFL's Super Bowl concert there. FIFA — EA's former licensing partner — built FIFA Super Soccer on the platform, past a billion visits. 

None of this is the board format in question, but it closes off the easiest explanation for EA's absence from it: access and brand safety are solved at every tier of this platform, not only at the board tier.

## What A Sweep Can Show, And What Only EA Can Answer
A check of the top football experiences and the partnership rosters of the studios that build them turns up no board carrying EA's brand and no EA integration. That holds the finding to no visible practice — a sweep, not a subpoena. 

Programmatic buys aren't published, so absence from public view isn't absence from a media plan. That gap is why this belongs in a meeting rather than a report: a brand can check what is visible, but only EA can say what it has actually bought.

## Three Answers Worth Testing In The Room
If EA states the audience is too young to justify the spend, that is worth testing against EA's own pipeline anxiety, its free-to-play EA SPORTS FC Mobile, and against Roblox's rule gating paid ad units to users thirteen and up. The claim, tested, turns out to be about teenagers, not young children.

If EA responds fan content isn't brand-safe, that holds against raw fan builds and dissolves against studio-operated ones — where the audience is largest and the same board format runs.

If EA says it won't fund a rival's ad business, that is the strongest of the three, and deserves to be granted before it's narrowed. EA buys media on YouTube, TikTok, and Twitch, every one a rival for the same hours, without hesitation. Once rivalry is ruled out as EA's actual policy, what's left is EA declining the identical format it sells Visa, Lowe's Companies, Inc., and State Farm at a premium — the one time the game isn't EA's own to price. A seller who won't buy the format for itself has already answered whether the premium is worth paying.

## A Second Question For The Same Meeting
A different shelf on the same platform carries a different, attributable format: rewarded video, an opt-in unit tracked to completion, buyable programmatically since 2025. Whether EA funds that unit is even harder to check than the board — buyer lists on programmatic exchanges aren't public, and the honest answer is not to guess. Either answer is still a finding. 

A clean no across both formats says the operator with the clearest motive left the whole rotation to everyone else. A quiet yes on the rewarded unit paired with a no on the board would reproduce, on someone else's platform, the same split the sibling piece found inside EA's own catalog: funding the format that tracks a response and skipping the one that doesn't.

## The Vacancy
A board's evidentiary limit does not change with the buyer. EA could check placements of its own games on its own boards because Madden and EA SPORTS FC's funnel belongs to EA — first-party data tying an exposure to a purchase the same account made. That capability doesn't travel. 

A board EA might buy on Roblox carries none of it, because that funnel belongs to Roblox, not EA. The same ceiling applies whether Visa buys the board, Lowe's buys it, or EA buys someone else's — display counts an exposure, not a response, regardless of whose name is on the invoice.

That is the vacancy underneath both questions, and it is a category problem, not an EA problem. The pitch for board space inside live play environments prices a premium no buyer can verify once purchased, because the format itself doesn't carry the evidence to verify it. EA's absence from its own pitch is not proof it checked and didn't like the answer. It is confirmation that nobody, including the operator who wrote the pitch, ever had a way to check.
