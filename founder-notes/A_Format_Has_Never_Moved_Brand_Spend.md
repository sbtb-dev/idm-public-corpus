# A Format Has Never Moved Brand Spend

Brand spend has never grown significantly inside an existing channel because a new format arrived. It has grown when a new line appeared with its own unit of evidence, and the formats inside that line inherited its legitimacy. The format has never created the line. Gaming's endemic actors are waiting for the first thing to happen. Every precedent is the second.

Last week supplied a fresh example of the expectation. Canva added gaming advertising to its media-buying platform through Livewire. The trade coverage called it growth. The seller's own description says otherwise.

## The seller described the unit
Inside Canva, a marketer selects dates, an audience, a budget, and a creative. Livewire returns an estimated reach before the campaign is booked, then buys, delivers, and optimizes. The formats are in-game billboards and interstitial banners served between gameplay. The unit sold is an impression. A board in a mobile racing game and a full-screen banner between levels produce the same evidentiary object as a display unit anywhere else. The IAB's own gaming framework routes both to display and measures them against display's standards. The routing was settled before the plugin existed.

The seller also described the tier it did not touch. Livewire states that much of the gaming market is still bought through managed campaigns, with bespoke deals, custom formats, and publisher-by-publisher negotiation. That is a description of a market with no unit. A buy that has to be assembled by hand every time is a buy for which nothing standard exists to purchase. In other words, the tier that carries the category's value has no line, no rate, and no comparable bid. The plugin sells the tier that already had all three.

## What growth looks like 
A growth story is a line a vendor breaks out because new demand arrived and the market will value knowing it. AppLovin supplies the control case. Its advertising business runs on display and video inventory inside mobile games, and its 2025 revenue reached $5.48 billion. That revenue is denominated per install. The unit priced is a response, and both sides observe it: the network through its own software in the app, the studio through its own attribution. A studio buying installs in another studio's game is not buying an audience. It is buying an outcome it can verify.

Non-endemic money arrived, and it arrived on the same terms. E-commerce advertisers now buy the same interstitials and rewarded video through AppLovin against a web purchase they observe with a pixel. The company broke that spend out the moment it was material, and reports it at roughly a billion dollars a year. The line appeared when the demand did, and the demand came with its own outcome attached.

No mobile ad network has ever broken out spend bought on the impression alone. That's because there is nothing to headline. An interstitial bought for reach is display, competes with display on price and targeting. In other words, every dollar that has ever scaled inside game environments scaled on an observable outcome. None of it scaled on the audience. The revenue is where the response is, and it stops where the response stops.

## Where brand spend has actually moved
Retail media grew from nothing to one of the largest lines in digital on display units indistinguishable from a banner. The format did not change. The evidence did. The placement sat inside an environment that observed the purchase, so the unit sold was an attributed sale. A new line appeared, defined by what the environment could see, and the money followed the line.

Podcast advertising ran for years as a direct-response (DR) channel. Host-read ads carried promo codes and vanity URLs because those were the only response evidence available, so DR advertisers bought and brands did not. Brand spend arrived after the unit did: a certified download count, then attribution. The format was the same host-read ad on both sides of that transition.

Skippable pre-roll grew brand video on YouTube, and it reads as a format innovation. It was a unit innovation. The advertiser paid only for a completed or chosen view. Remove the pricing change and skippable pre-roll is pre-roll people leave.

Rewarded video transformed game advertising. It did so for buyers who could observe their outcome: a studio watching for the install, later an e-commerce advertiser watching for the purchase. The format enabled the evidence, and the evidence carried the spend. Spend that could not observe its outcome did not follow.

## Where it didn't
Rich media and expandable banners were built to bring brand budgets to display. They did not. Display stayed a performance channel and brand money stayed in television. Native display, high-impact takeovers, six-second bumpers, shoppable and QR-enabled television spots: each was a better format than what it replaced, and each left the evidence class unchanged. Better formats with the same evidence moved no budgets. Rich media has had twenty years.

## The nearest counterexample
Influencer marketing grew large with weak measurement, inside social, and looks like a format that built its own spend. It fits the pattern on inspection. Its early growth was carried by affiliate codes and direct response, the podcast pattern repeated. It rode the one channel whose spend was already the fastest-growing in media. And the market now tracks it as its own line regardless of taxonomy. Response evidence first, then a line, then brand spend. The order held.

## What the pattern says
Brand spend has grown in exactly one way. A line appears with its own evidence terms, and the formats inside it become legitimate by inheritance. Where a new format appears to have driven growth, the format was the vehicle for a new unit the buyer could pay for. A new unit is a new evidence class. Nothing else has ever done the job.

Endemic actors expecting gaming to grow on boards, playables, and interactive units are betting on the rich-media outcome while citing the retail-media one. The retail-media outcome required a new line, defined by what the environment observes, held to a standard the seller does not control. That is the thing the format-led expectation is built to avoid.

## What is still missing
The install and the pixel close a loop at conversion. The environments behind these deals observe more than that: activation, engagement, retention, and monetization, natively, because their business model cannot run otherwise. 

Immersive media is one gaming-related spend that has no line in any book. It is advertising that produces direct behavioral evidence of consumer response at pre-conversion funnel stages, captured by the environment's native instrumentation, attributable to the brand's presence. Display cannot produce it. Video cannot produce it. A plugin that sells display across a pool of games cannot produce it.

The gaming bundle grew again. The real growth story is waiting for a new immersive media line.
